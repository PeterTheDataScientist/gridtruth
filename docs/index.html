Nobody publishes Zimbabwe's load shedding schedule.

Not on ZETDC's website, anyway. This page checks, continuously, and records what it finds. When a schedule does appear it will be archived here permanently.

Why this exists

ZETDC's own load shedding FAQ answers the question "Why is ZETDC not sticking to the schedules it advertised?" like this:

“…loading will be done outside the programme without notice.”

ZETDC, Load Shedding FAQs

So the utility states plainly that unannounced load shedding happens. It does not publish the programme those cuts depart from, and no public record exists of how often reality and announcement diverge.

That is the gap. An announcement you cannot check is not accountability, and a cut nobody recorded did not happen as far as any dataset is concerned. The next stage of this project measures the cuts directly, from satellite night lights, so the observation does not depend on the utility publishing anything at all.

What this does not do yet

  • It cannot tell you if your power is about to go off. No schedule is published to tell you from.
  • The satellite verification is not built yet. The method, its failure modes and a pre-registered accuracy gate are written up in DESIGN.md and EVALUATION.md, before any result exists.
  • Crowdsourced reports are not open yet. When they are, they will be one signal of three, never a ground truth.
  • Coverage is three ZETDC pages. If you know where schedules are actually published, that is the single most useful thing you could tell this project. Open an issue.
